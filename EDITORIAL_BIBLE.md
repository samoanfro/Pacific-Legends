# Editorial Bible

## The Central Rule

Every story must increase respect for the people and cultures of the Pacific.

## Cultural Framing

Polynesia is not one culture. Each episode must identify the island group, historical setting, language context, social structure, and cultural references being used.

When a story is fictional, say so.

When a story is based on oral tradition, say so.

When historians disagree, say so.

## Strength and Pain Tolerance

Stories may use historical rumors, legends, and outsider observations describing Polynesian people as physically imposing, disciplined, athletic, brave, or difficult to stop in battle.

However:

- Do not make biological superiority claims.
- Do not claim all Polynesian people have unusually high pain tolerance.
- Frame extraordinary endurance as a combination of training, duty, social expectations, environment, courage, and individual character.
- Pair physical strength with responsibility.
- The strongest character should not automatically be the wisest.
- Avoid making violence the only proof of worth.

## Women

Women may be warriors, chiefs, chiefesses, strategists, navigators, healers, landholders, diplomats, mothers, teachers, and protectors.

Do not make every woman character prove herself to men.

Some stories may involve outsiders misjudging a woman, but her identity should be deeper than the reveal.

## Fa'afafine Story Standard

A fa'afafine character may appear when the story naturally supports:

- Family
- Service
- Caregiving
- Courage
- Leadership
- Humor
- Cultural knowledge
- Mediation
- Community survival

Do not frame the character through victimhood.

Do not write the story as a modern political argument.

Do not treat cross-gender presentation as a joke, twist, scandal, or deception.

The character should already belong to the family and community. Conflict should arise from the wider story, not from forcing a rejection narrative.

## Samoan and Tongan Conflict

Both sides must contain fully human characters.

Avoid:

- “Good Samoans versus evil Tongans”
- “Superior Tongans versus weak Samoans”
- Internet rivalry bait presented as history

Use:

- Rival leaders
- Competing obligations
- Courage on both sides
- Respect between warriors
- Political alliances
- Marriages and dynasties
- Shared ocean culture
- Different perspectives on the same event

## Europeans

European characters may be arrogant, compassionate, frightened, greedy, brave, foolish, honorable, or conflicted.

Do not make every European a villain.

The strongest formula is misunderstanding, danger, revelation, and earned respect.

## Religious Material

Missionaries may appear, but stories should avoid blanket attacks on Christianity or Indigenous belief.

Where beliefs differ, show human motives and historical tension without cheap insults.

## Historical Disclosure Labels

Use one of these at the beginning or end:

### Historical Fiction
An original fictional story inspired by real Pacific history and culture.

### Inspired by Oral Tradition
A dramatized story inspired by oral accounts that may have multiple versions.

### Historical Retelling
A dramatized retelling based primarily on documented events, with invented dialogue and compressed timelines.

### Legend
A cinematic retelling of a traditional or legendary account.
