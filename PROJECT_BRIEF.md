# Pacific Legends — Codex Project Brief

## Project Objective

Build a repeatable content-production system that creates finished, upload-ready YouTube storytelling packages based on original historical fiction inspired by Polynesian cultures.

The system should help a human producer move from story idea to finished video while maintaining:

- Strong hooks and audience retention
- Original scripts
- Consistent characters
- Historically believable visuals
- Cultural respect
- Clear separation between documented history, oral tradition, legend, and fiction
- Reusable production templates
- Monetizable long-form content
- A sustainable release cadence

## Audience

Primary audience:

- Adults 30–70
- History and adventure fans
- Polynesian communities and diaspora
- Military, sports, rugby, and football audiences
- Viewers who enjoy HFY, historical fiction, survival stories, and moral storytelling
- Families who enjoy stories about honor, sacrifice, love, courage, and legacy

## Brand Positioning

Pacific Legends is not a documentary channel and should not pretend that fictional dialogue or invented characters are documented history.

It is a cinematic historical-fiction channel inspired by the people, cultures, histories, and oral traditions of the Pacific.

## Story Pillars

1. Warriors and protectors
2. Women warriors, chiefesses, queens, navigators, healers, and strategists
3. European ships arriving in the islands
4. Ocean navigation and survival
5. Chiefs, diplomacy, justice, and leadership
6. Family, marriage, dignity, and transformation
7. Samoan and Tongan conflict, rivalry, alliance, and shared respect
8. Legends and oral traditions
9. Family and belonging, including a respectful fa'afafine-centered story when naturally connected to service, courage, wisdom, family, or community
10. Historical events retold through grounded fictional characters

## Required Emotional Pattern

Most episodes should include:

1. A rumor, warning, or assumption
2. A person who dismisses it
3. A clash of values or misunderstanding
4. Rising danger
5. A sacrifice or difficult choice
6. A reveal
7. Earned respect
8. A strong final line
9. A fact-versus-fiction note

## Tone

- Cinematic
- Serious but accessible
- Emotional without becoming melodramatic
- Respectful of all Pacific cultures
- Never anti-European
- Never based on racial superiority
- Strength should be paired with discipline, service, loyalty, sacrifice, or wisdom
- Do not use modern political language inside historical stories
- Do not reduce Polynesian characters to size, violence, tattoos, or athletic ability

## Technical Output Requirements

Each episode package should contain:

- `script.md`
- `scene_plan.csv`
- `image_prompts.md`
- `character_refs.md`
- `thumbnail_brief.md`
- `youtube_metadata.md`
- `shorts.md`
- `historical_notes.md`
- `production_manifest.json`

## Long-Form Specifications

- Target runtime: 12–18 minutes
- Target narration length: 2,000–3,000 words
- Average narration pace: 140–155 words per minute
- Opening hook: first 15 seconds
- First major reversal: before 3:00
- Major reveal or sacrifice: approximately 65–80% into the story
- Closing historical note: 20–45 seconds

## Visual Specifications

- 16:9 landscape
- Cinematic realism
- Historically plausible 1700s–1800s setting
- No modern objects
- No random pan-Polynesian mixing without explanation
- Character consistency across images
- Use visual changes every 5–10 seconds
- Do not require a unique still for every sentence
- Reuse establishing shots with controlled motion when appropriate
- Aim for 35–55 master images per 12–18 minute episode
- Create additional close-up crops and motion variants in editing

## Content Safety and Integrity

Avoid:

- Invented claims presented as fact
- Sacred practices used as cheap horror
- Polynesian cultures blended into one generic culture
- One-sided ethnic rivalry
- Graphic gore
- Sexualized depictions
- Mockery of language, names, customs, or faith
- Repetitive AI-generated filler

## Definition of Done

An episode is complete only when a producer can:

1. Generate or source the images.
2. Generate the narration.
3. Assemble the timeline.
4. Add music and sound effects.
5. Export the video.
6. Upload using the supplied title, description, thumbnail, chapters, and tags.
